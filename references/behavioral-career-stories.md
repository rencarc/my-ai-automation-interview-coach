# Behavioral And Career Stories

Use this for behavioral interviews, career-switch narrative, stakeholder communication, ownership, ambiguity, and internship/project story preparation.

## Core Narrative

The user's story should be:

> I started in business operations, where I learned how real workflows, customers, suppliers, and stakeholders behave. During my MSc in Information Security, I moved into applied AI and automation. Now I build AI workflow systems that are technically sound but grounded in business process reality.

This is a strength, not an apology. The business background explains why the user can identify useful automation opportunities and communicate with non-technical stakeholders.

## STAR Plus Reflection

Use:

```text
Situation:
Task:
Action:
Result:
Reflection:
```

For senior-leaning roles, add:

```text
Tradeoff:
What I would do differently:
How it changed my approach:
```

## Mature Behavioral Answer Checks

Before accepting an answer as interview-ready, check:

- Does it include a concrete result, not just "it went well"?
- Does it balance "I" and "we" so the user's ownership is clear without sounding like a lone hero?
- Does it include a tradeoff, constraint, or conflict?
- Does it explain stakeholder impact?
- Does it include a reflection or change in future behavior?
- Is the story calibrated to the role level?

If any item is missing, ask a follow-up before rewriting the answer.

## Likely Questions

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Why AI automation?
3. Why should we hire you for this role?
4. Tell me about a project you are proud of.
5. Tell me about a time you handled ambiguity.
6. Tell me about a time you worked with non-technical stakeholders.
7. Tell me about a time something did not work as expected.
8. Tell me about a technical tradeoff you made.
9. How does your business background help you as a developer?
10. What are you learning now?

## Red Flags To Coach Out

- **Framework recitation**: saying STAR mechanically instead of telling a clear story.
- **Missing result**: no metric, observable change, or learning.
- **Tool-list answer**: "I used Copilot Studio and Power Automate" without problem, action, and impact.
- **Bluffing**: pretending to know a domain instead of reasoning from first principles.
- **No tradeoff**: every decision sounds obvious and risk-free.
- **Generic questions for interviewer**: prepare role-specific questions that are not answered on the company website.

## Strong Interviewer Questions To Ask At The End

- "For this role, what separates someone who is productive in three months from someone who struggles?"
- "Where is the team currently using AI automation, and where has adoption been harder than expected?"
- "How do you evaluate quality and risk for AI-generated workflow actions?"
- "What is the biggest integration or data-governance challenge this team is dealing with?"
- "If I joined and after six months you felt I was not meeting expectations, what would most likely be the reason?"

## Answer Positioning

Emphasize:

- applied AI, not just experimentation,
- workflow and stakeholder understanding,
- security-aware design,
- ability to build across low-code and custom-code stacks,
- learning speed and practical ownership.

Avoid:

- sounding like the business background is unrelated,
- over-claiming senior engineering depth,
- describing projects as tool lists instead of decisions and outcomes.
